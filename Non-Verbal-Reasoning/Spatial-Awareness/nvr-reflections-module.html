1. What is Reflection?

0/8 done ✓
Simple Explanation

Imagine you're holding a shape up to a mirror. The mirror shows you the shape flipped — like how your left hand looks like a right hand in the mirror. The shape doesn't spin or move sideways — it just gets flipped. Whatever is close to the mirror stays close on the other side. Whatever is far away stays far away.

Full Explanation

Reflection is a transformation where every point on a shape is mapped to a corresponding point on the opposite side of the mirror line (also called the axis of reflection), at an equal perpendicular distance. The reflected image is the same size and shape as the original, but its orientation is reversed — left becomes right (for a vertical axis) or top becomes bottom (for a horizontal axis).

Worked Example — Easy
A triangle pointing RIGHT is reflected across a vertical mirror line. What does the reflection look like?
Original (points →)
Reflection (points ←)
  • Find the mirror line — it's the vertical dashed red line.
  • The tip of the triangle is on the RIGHT side of the original. After reflection, the tip must be on the LEFT side.
  • The flat base was on the left. After reflection, it goes to the right.
  • Near stays near, far stays far — the distance from the mirror is preserved on both sides.
✓ Answer: Triangle pointing LEFT — the tip has flipped to the other side of the mirror.
⚠️ Watch Out!

The most common mistake is choosing a shape that is identical to the original (no flip at all) or a rotation. A reflection always reverses the facing direction — like a left shoe vs a right shoe. If it looks exactly the same as the original, it's wrong!

Worked Example — Harder
A right-angled triangle has its right angle at the bottom-left. It is reflected across a vertical mirror. Where is the right angle in the reflection?
  • Identify the key feature: the right angle is at bottom-LEFT of the original.
  • After a vertical reflection, LEFT and RIGHT swap — but UP and DOWN stay the same.
  • So the right angle moves from bottom-LEFT to bottom-RIGHT.
  • Eliminate any option where the right angle is on the left or at the top.
✓ Answer: Right angle at bottom-RIGHT. The vertical mirror swapped left and right only.

2. Identifying Mirror Direction

0/8 done ✓
Simple Explanation

The mirror can stand upright (vertical) like a bathroom mirror, or lie flat (horizontal) like a pond reflecting trees, or lean diagonally like a tilted frame. Each type flips the shape differently. Vertical mirror → left and right swap. Horizontal mirror → top and bottom swap. Diagonal mirrors swap both at once in a tricky way.

Full Explanation

The axis of reflection determines which dimension is reversed. A vertical axis (|) reverses horizontal position — left becomes right. A horizontal axis (—) reverses vertical position — top becomes bottom. A diagonal axis at 45° swaps x and y coordinates simultaneously, which also reverses the orientation of angled lines. Students must identify the mirror direction before attempting to reflect any feature.

Worked Example — Easy
A right-angled triangle has its right angle at the top-right. The mirror is horizontal. Where is the right angle in the reflection?
  • Identify mirror type: HORIZONTAL — this swaps top and bottom, but keeps left and right the same.
  • Right angle was at TOP-right. After a horizontal mirror, TOP becomes BOTTOM.
  • LEFT/RIGHT stays the same — so "right" stays "right".
  • Result: right angle is now at BOTTOM-right.
✓ Answer: Right angle at bottom-right. Only top/bottom swapped.
⚠️ Watch Out!

Don't confuse mirror direction with what gets flipped. A VERTICAL mirror flips LEFT↔RIGHT (not up/down). A HORIZONTAL mirror flips TOP↔BOTTOM (not left/right). Students often apply the wrong swap. Always ask: "Is the mirror standing up or lying flat?"

Worked Example — Harder
A shape has a shaded triangle in the top-left corner and a circle near the bottom-right. The mirror is horizontal. Describe the correct reflection.
  • Horizontal mirror: top↔bottom swap. Left and right stay the same.
  • Shaded triangle was at TOP-left. After reflection: BOTTOM-left.
  • Circle was near BOTTOM-right. After reflection: TOP-right.
  • Check: left/right positions are unchanged. Only up/down flipped.
✓ Answer: Shaded triangle at bottom-left, circle at top-right.

3. Offset Reflections (Distance from Mirror)

0/8 done ✓
Simple Explanation

Imagine a swimmer standing 3 metres from a pool edge. Their reflection in the water also appears 3 metres deep. The distance from the mirror is always the same on both sides. A shape close to the mirror reflects close. A shape far from the mirror reflects far. The gap is copied exactly.

Full Explanation

In offset reflections, the shape does not touch the mirror line. Each point on the shape is at a specific perpendicular distance from the mirror. Its reflection is placed at the same perpendicular distance on the opposite side. Students must track both the flip of orientation AND the preservation of distance. A common error is placing the reflected shape touching the mirror even when the original was far from it.

Worked Example — Easy
A square is 4 grid squares to the LEFT of a vertical mirror. Where does the reflection appear?
  • The shape is 4 squares to the LEFT of the mirror.
  • The reflection appears on the RIGHT side of the mirror.
  • The distance is preserved: the reflection is also 4 squares from the mirror.
  • So the reflection is 4 squares to the RIGHT of the mirror line.
✓ Answer: The square appears 4 grid squares to the RIGHT of the mirror.
⚠️ Watch Out!

The trap here is choosing the reflection right next to the mirror even when the original is far away. Always count the distance! If the shape is 3 squares from the mirror, the reflection must also be exactly 3 squares from the mirror on the other side — not 1, not 5.

Worked Example — Harder
A triangle sits 2 grid squares to the RIGHT of a vertical mirror with its tip pointing UP-RIGHT. Which is the correct reflection?
  • Distance: 2 squares from mirror. Reflection must also be 2 squares from mirror — on the LEFT side.
  • Orientation flip: vertical mirror swaps left/right. Tip pointed UP-RIGHT. RIGHT becomes LEFT. So tip now points UP-LEFT.
  • Eliminate option: any reflection touching the mirror (wrong distance) → GONE.
  • Eliminate option: any reflection with tip pointing UP-RIGHT (no flip) → GONE.
  • Correct: triangle 2 squares to the LEFT, tip pointing UP-LEFT.
✓ Answer: Triangle 2 squares to the left of the mirror, tip pointing up-left.

4. Diagonal Lines in Reflections

0/8 done ✓
Simple Explanation

Diagonal lines are the trickiest part of reflections. When you reflect a line that slopes up-to-the-right (like \), it becomes a line that slopes up-to-the-left (like /). The angle stays the same but the TILT DIRECTION flips. Think of it like a slide: if the slide goes down to the right, its reflection goes down to the left.

Full Explanation

Diagonal lines undergo angle inversion when reflected. A line slanting at +45° (rising left to right, like /) reflects to a line at −45° (falling left to right, like \) across a vertical axis. The magnitude of the angle from the vertical is preserved, but the direction (left or right lean) is reversed. This is the most common discriminator in GL assessment options — two shapes can look similar, but opposite diagonal lean directions.

Worked Example — Easy
A line slopes from bottom-left to top-right ( / shape). It is reflected across a vertical mirror. What does the reflected line look like?
  • Original line: slopes UP going from left to right — the "/" direction.
  • Vertical mirror flips left↔right.
  • After flipping: the high end was on the RIGHT, now it must be on the LEFT.
  • So the line now slopes UP going from right to left — which means it goes "\" direction.
✓ Answer: The line changes from / to \ direction.
⚠️ Watch Out!

The most dangerous trap: students think "same angle = correct reflection." Wrong! A reflected diagonal must change lean direction across a vertical mirror. If the original leans right, the reflection leans left. If you see an option with the same diagonal direction as the original, eliminate it immediately.

Worked Example — Harder
A triangle has a base at the bottom (horizontal) and a slanted left edge going from bottom-left to top-center ( the left edge is a / slope). It is reflected across a vertical mirror. What does the reflection look like?
  • Step 0: Eliminate any option that looks identical to the original — that's just a copy, not a reflection.
  • The base is horizontal. Horizontal lines are unchanged by a vertical mirror — the base stays horizontal.
  • The left slanting edge was a "/" slope (up-left to up-center). After vertical reflection, left↔right swap. The slant becomes "\" (up-right going toward center).
  • In the original, the tall peak was toward the center-top. In the reflection, the peak is still at the top-center (center is symmetric). But the slant directions of the two sides switch.
  • The reflected triangle: base still at bottom, but left and right sides are swapped — the steep/slanted sides reverse.
✓ Answer: The triangle is the mirror image — its slanted sides have swapped, even though the base remains horizontal.

5. Hidden Mirror Lines

0/8 done ✓
Simple Explanation

Sometimes in exam questions, the dashed mirror line is not shown. You have to figure out where the mirror must be just by looking at the shape and its reflection. The trick is to find the middle point between any part of the original and the same part of the reflection — the mirror always runs through that middle point.

Full Explanation

When the axis of reflection is not explicitly shown, students must infer its position and orientation. The mirror line is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining any point on the original to its corresponding point on the reflection. To find it: pick a recognisable feature (e.g. a corner, a dot), find the equivalent feature in the reflection, and locate the midpoint between them — the mirror passes through that midpoint, perpendicular to the line joining the two features.

Worked Example — Easy
A triangle's tip is at position (2, 5) on a grid. The reflection's tip is at (8, 5). Find the mirror line.
  • Both tips are at the same height (y=5) — so the mirror is vertical (it doesn't tilt).
  • The two tips are at x=2 and x=8. The midpoint is (2+8)÷2 = 5.
  • So the mirror line is the vertical line x=5, running between the two shapes.
  • Check: distance from x=2 to x=5 is 3. Distance from x=5 to x=8 is 3. ✓ Equal distances.
✓ Answer: Vertical mirror at x=5 (the midpoint between the two corresponding tips).
⚠️ Watch Out!

Don't assume the mirror is always vertical! Look at the relationship between the original and reflection. If one is directly above the other, the mirror is horizontal. If they're side by side, the mirror is vertical. If they're diagonal from each other — check carefully for a diagonal mirror.

Worked Example — Harder
You see two L-shapes on a grid. One L opens to the right. The other opens upward and appears diagonally across. What type of mirror is this?
  • An L opening right reflected across a VERTICAL mirror → L opening left (not upward).
  • An L opening right reflected across a HORIZONTAL mirror → L opening right but flipped vertically (still opens right).
  • An L opening right reflected across a 45° DIAGONAL mirror → the horizontal and vertical arms swap. The L opens upward.
  • The shapes are arranged diagonally from each other AND one opens upward (rotated 90° from the original). This matches a 45° diagonal mirror.
✓ Answer: 45° diagonal mirror. A diagonal mirror swaps x and y coordinates, turning an L on its side.

6. Multi-Feature Shapes

0/8 done ✓
Simple Explanation

Some shapes have several things to track at once: shading, arrows, dots, stripes, and different line angles. The trick is to check features one at a time, in order of importance. Start with the most unusual or easy-to-spot feature. Check it first. If an option gets that feature wrong, cross it out immediately and move on.

Full Explanation

Multi-feature reflections require systematic feature prioritisation. Not all features are equally discriminating. Arrows and directional indicators are most useful because they have an obvious orientation. Shading regions follow next. Dots and small marks are useful for distance verification. Students should apply the Point-Reach Method: identify the most distinctive feature, check its reflected position and orientation, eliminate wrong options, then verify secondary features.

Worked Example — Easy
A rectangle has: a black dot in the top-left corner, diagonal shading on the right half, and an arrow pointing right. Vertical mirror. What does the reflection look like?
  • Step 0: Eliminate any option that looks identical (no flip). And any rotation.
  • Arrow: was pointing RIGHT →. Vertical mirror flips left/right. Arrow now points LEFT ←. Eliminate any option where arrow still points right.
  • Dot: was in TOP-LEFT. Vertical mirror: left↔right. Dot moves to TOP-RIGHT.
  • Shading: was on the RIGHT half. After vertical flip: shading moves to the LEFT half.
  • Check all three features in the correct answer: arrow left, dot top-right, shading on left half.
✓ Answer: Arrow points left, dot in top-right, shading on left half.
⚠️ Watch Out!

Central fixation error: students focus only on the middle of the shape and miss edge features like dots in corners or shading along a border. Always scan the entire shape — corners and edges are where exam traps hide.

Worked Example — Harder (GL Trap Level)
A rectangle split diagonally (\). Left triangle: shaded. Right triangle: white. A small arrow points toward top-right (↗). A semicircle sits on the left edge. Vertical mirror. Which is correct?
  • Step 0: Eliminate identical copies and rotations immediately.
  • Diagonal \: across a vertical mirror, \ becomes /. Any option with \ is wrong.
  • Arrow ↗: after vertical mirror, left↔right. ↗ becomes ↖. Any option with ↗ or ↙ is wrong.
  • Semicircle on LEFT edge: left↔right. Semicircle moves to the RIGHT edge. Eliminate any option with semicircle still on left.
  • Shading: was in the LEFT triangle (to the left of the \). After flip — the shading stays in the same relative triangle (now to the right of the / diagonal), meaning shading is in the RIGHT triangle.
  • Correct answer: / diagonal, arrow ↖, semicircle on right edge, right triangle shaded.
✓ Answer: / diagonal, arrow pointing top-left (↖), semicircle on right edge, right side shaded.

7. Common Traps & Misconceptions

0/8 done ✓
Simple Explanation

GL examiners know exactly where students go wrong, and they put those wrong answers as options to trap you. The four biggest traps are: (1) picking the identical shape, (2) picking a rotation instead of a reflection, (3) getting the diagonal direction wrong, and (4) forgetting to check the distance from the mirror. Know the traps, beat the traps.

Full Explanation

The elimination strategy for reflections: Step 0 — immediately eliminate identical copies and rotations. Then check nearest distinctive feature orientation. Then check diagonal directions (critical discriminator). Then verify distance from mirror. Finally confirm all minor details. This ordered process prevents the most common errors: choosing unchecked identical-looking distractors and failing to notice angle inversions.

Elimination Table — Quick Reference
TrapHow to Spot ItEliminate?
Identical copy (no flip)Looks exactly like the original✗ Eliminate
RotationShape turned, not flipped — check if it could be a rotation✗ Eliminate
Wrong diagonal direction\ should become / or vice versa✗ Eliminate
Wrong distance from mirrorShape too close or too far from mirror✗ Eliminate
Correct on all countsPassed all checks above✓ Select
⚠️ Watch Out!

"It looks right" is not enough. Always run through the checklist. GL questions are designed so that a "close but wrong" option looks visually convincing. The difference might be a single diagonal line direction or one dot in the wrong corner.

Worked Example — Trap Spotting
You see 4 options. Original: triangle pointing up-right with a \ diagonal. Vertical mirror. Spot the trap options.
  • Option A: Triangle pointing up-right, \ diagonal → IDENTICAL COPY. Eliminate immediately.
  • Option B: Triangle rotated 90° clockwise → ROTATION not reflection. Eliminate.
  • Option C: Triangle pointing up-left, \ diagonal → DIAGONAL WRONG (should be /). Eliminate.
  • Option D: Triangle pointing up-left, / diagonal → Correct flip, correct diagonal. Keep.
✓ Answer: D. Every other option contained a deliberate trap.
