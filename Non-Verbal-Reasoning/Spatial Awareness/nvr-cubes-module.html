Adjacency — Which Faces Touch?
✓
🧒 Simple Explanation

Imagine a cube like a dice. It has 6 faces. Adjacent just means "touching" — like neighbours on a street. If two faces are next to each other on the cube, they are adjacent. If a face is on the opposite side, it cannot be adjacent to the other face — ever! Think of it like front and back: your face and the back of your head can never be next to each other.

📖 Full Explanation

A cube has 6 faces. Each face has exactly 4 adjacent (touching) faces and 1 opposite face. Two faces that are opposite can never be adjacent. In exam questions, you are shown a cube image with 3 visible faces and must judge whether a given cube could exist based on which faces touch which.


Key rule: On any cube, if you can see 3 faces at once, none of those 3 faces are opposite to each other — they are all adjacent.

📐 Worked Example 1 — Basic

Question: A cube is shown with ⭐ on top, 🔴 on the front, and 🔵 on the right. Can ⭐ and 🔴 be opposite faces?

Look at the cube. You can see 3 faces at once: ⭐, 🔴, 🔵.

Apply the rule. Any 3 visible faces are all touching each other — none of them are opposite.

Answer. No — ⭐ and 🔴 are adjacent (touching), not opposite.

⚠️ Watch Out!

Most common mistake: Students assume that because two symbols look "far apart" in the image, they must be opposite. They're not! All 3 visible faces are always adjacent. If it's on the same image of the cube, it's touching.

📐 Worked Example 2 — Harder

Question: Cube 1 shows: Top = A, Front = B, Right = C. Cube 2 shows: Top = A, Front = C, Right = D. Which faces are definitely adjacent to A?

From Cube 1: A touches B and C (they are all visible together).

From Cube 2: A touches C and D.

Combining: A is adjacent to B, C, and D. Since a cube has 4 adjacent faces, only one face is still unknown as adjacent to A.

Conclusion: B, C, and D are all adjacent to A. The face NOT seen is opposite to A.

Opposite Faces — Deduction & Common Face Method
✓
🧒 Simple Explanation

Think of a dice: the 1 is always opposite the 6, the 2 opposite the 5, and the 3 opposite the 4. That's 3 pairs of opposites. On your cube in the exam, you need to figure out these pairs like a detective. The trick? Use the "common face" — find a face that appears in two different pictures, and use it as your anchor to work out what it's not touching.

📖 Full Explanation

A cube has 3 opposite pairs. Once you know all 3 pairs, you can solve any cube question. The Common Face Method works like this:

  1. Find a face that appears in two different cube views.
  2. List all faces it touches in each view.
  3. Any face that is never shown adjacent to it — and not the common face itself — is its opposite.

Three Faces Never Include Opposites: If you can see 3 faces simultaneously, none of them are opposite. This is an absolute rule — never forget it.

📐 Worked Example 1 — Common Face Method

Given: View 1: Top=A, Front=B, Right=C. View 2: Top=A, Front=D, Right=E. Faces: A,B,C,D,E,F. Find: What is opposite A?

A is the common face. In View 1, A is adjacent to B and C. In View 2, A is adjacent to D and E.

A is adjacent to: B, C, D, E (4 faces — that's all 4 of A's adjacent faces).

The only face not yet listed is F. F is not adjacent to A.

Answer: Opposite of A = F. ✓

A ↔ F
⚠️ Watch Out!

Most common mistake: Students confuse "never seen together" with "definitely opposite." Two faces might not appear in the same view simply because we haven't been shown that view yet — that alone doesn't prove they're opposite. Always work through the adjacency logic step by step.

📐 Worked Example 2 — Full Opposite Pair Deduction

Given: View 1: Top=☀️, Front=🌙, Right=⭐. View 2: Top=☀️, Front=⭐, Right=🌊. View 3: Top=🌙, Front=⭐, Right=🔥. Faces: ☀️,🌙,⭐,🌊,🔥,❄️. Find all 3 opposite pairs.

☀️ appears in V1 and V2. Adjacent to: 🌙,⭐ (V1) and ⭐,🌊 (V2). So ☀️ adj to 🌙,⭐,🌊. Missing from cube face list: 🔥,❄️.

From V3, 🌙 adj to ⭐,🔥. So 🌙 is adjacent to ⭐ and 🔥. Combined: 🌙 adj to ☀️,⭐,🔥. Missing: 🌊,❄️. Which is opposite 🌙? Since 🌙 adj ☀️ — ☀️ is not opposite 🌙. 🌊 or ❄️ must be opposite 🌙.

Orientation — Which Way is the Symbol Facing?
✓
🧒 Simple Explanation

Imagine a dice with an arrow on one face. Even if you rotate the dice, that arrow still has a direction — it points somewhere specific. Orientation is about which way a symbol faces, not just which symbol it is. Two cubes might have all the same symbols on all the right faces, but if one arrow points left and the other points right, they are different cubes. It's like the difference between a 'b' and a 'd' — same shape, wrong direction!

📖 Full Explanation

Orientation refers to the rotational direction of a symbol on a cube face. After confirming that the correct symbol is on the correct face (adjacency check), you must verify that the symbol is pointing in the correct direction. A rotation of the cube moves all faces together, but a symbol facing the wrong way cannot be corrected by any valid cube rotation — it would require a reflection (flipping).

Key rule: Rotation ≠ Reflection. A cube can be rotated to any orientation, but you can never "mirror" it. If a symbol is facing the wrong way, the cube is invalid.

📐 Worked Example 1

Question: Given cube shows ➡️ on the top face pointing towards you. An option shows the same face on top, but the arrow ⬅️ points away. Is this a valid rotation?

Check: same symbol? Yes — arrow present on top. ✓

Check: orientation? The original arrow points towards viewer; option points away — this is a 180° flip of the symbol direction.

Can a cube rotation flip a symbol's direction like this? No — you can tilt and spin the cube, but you cannot mirror it.

Answer: Invalid cube. ✗

⚠️ Watch Out!

Most common mistake: Students focus only on whether the correct symbol is on the correct face, and forget to check its direction. This is the trap that catches even strong students. Always check both: right symbol AND right direction.

📐 Worked Example 2 — Harder

Question: The original cube has: Top=↗️ (arrow pointing top-right), Front=🔵, Right=🔴. Option A shows: Top=↙️, Front=🔵, Right=🔴. Option B shows: Top=↗️, Front=🔵, Right=🔴.

Check Option A: Symbol on top is arrow ✓, Front=🔵 ✓, Right=🔴 ✓. But top arrow direction: ↙️ vs original ↗️ — this is a 180° rotation of the symbol. Invalid!

Check Option B: Top=↗️ ✓ (correct direction), Front=🔵 ✓, Right=🔴 ✓. All correct!

Answer: Option B is the valid cube. ✓

Nets — Folding into a Cube
✓
🧒 Simple Explanation

A net is just a cube "unfolded flat" — like if you unpeeled all 6 faces and laid them out on a table. To solve net questions, imagine you're folding the flat shape back up into a box. The tricky bit: two faces that are opposite each other in the folded cube will be far apart on the net. Think of a cereal box cut open and flattened — the front and back are never sitting next to each other on the flat version!

📖 Full Explanation

A net is a 2D arrangement of 6 squares that folds into a cube. There are 11 valid net shapes for a cube. In GL Assessment, you must identify:

  • Which faces become opposite after folding
  • Whether a given net can produce a given cube
  • Whether a net is a valid cube net (not all 6-square arrangements fold into cubes)

Key rules for nets:

  • In a cross/T/L-shaped net, faces directly across from each other in the cross are usually opposite
  • Any net with 4+ squares in a line and 1 square on each of 2 sides is valid
  • A net with 4 squares in a 2×2 block is invalid (creates overlap)

Hidden Face Deduction
✓
🧒 Simple Explanation

Sometimes you're asked: "What's on the hidden face?" — the side you can't see. It's like a puzzle where you know all 6 faces of the cube, and you can see 3 of them. The hidden one you can't see must be the one that's opposite the face you CAN see on that side. Remember: every face has exactly one face directly across from it!

📖 Full Explanation

In hidden face questions, you are given either a cube image (showing 3 faces) and all 6 face labels, and asked to deduce what is on the face you cannot see — the bottom, the back, or the left face. Use these steps:

  1. Identify the 3 visible faces.
  2. Identify which face position is hidden (e.g. the bottom).
  3. The hidden face is the opposite of the visible face in that same axis (bottom = opposite of top).
  4. Use known opposite pairs (from the views given or the question) to state the answer.
📐 Worked Example 1

Given: All 6 faces: 🌟,🔴,🔵,🌙,⭐,🟣. Cube view shows Top=🌟, Front=🔴, Right=🔵. Opposite pairs (worked out): 🌟↔🟣, 🔴↔⭐, 🔵↔🌙. What is on the bottom face?

Top face = 🌟. The bottom is always opposite the top.

🌟's opposite = 🟣 (from known pairs).

Answer: Bottom face = 🟣 ✓

⚠️ Watch Out!

Most common mistake: Students list a face they can see in the image as the hidden face answer. If a face is already visible in the image, it cannot also be the hidden face — it's being used! The hidden face must come from the 3 faces you cannot see.

📐 Worked Example 2 — Harder: Using Multiple Views

Given: View 1: Top=A, Front=B, Right=C. View 2: Top=D, Front=B, Right=E. All faces: A,B,C,D,E,F. What is on the back face in View 1?

In View 1: visible = Top(A), Front(B), Right(C). Hidden faces = Bottom, Back, Left.

Back face is opposite Front face. Front = B. Need opposite of B.

From View 1: B adj A, C. From View 2: B adj D, E. So B adj A,C,D,E — 4 adjacent faces. Remaining face = F. F is opposite B.

Answer: Back face = F ✓

Same Cube vs Different Cube
✓
🧒 Simple Explanation

You're shown a cube and 4 options. You must find which option COULD be the same cube just rotated. Think of it like spinning a dice — the same dice, just turned. Your job is to spot which options are impossible (wrong face positions or wrong symbol directions) and eliminate them. The one that survives is your answer!

📖 Full Explanation

Same Cube questions give you a reference cube and ask which option could be the same cube in a different rotation. Use this 3-step elimination strategy:

  1. Check opposite pairs: If the reference cube tells you X and Y are opposite, eliminate any option showing X and Y adjacent.
  2. Check adjacency: Eliminate any option that shows a face adjacent to a face it cannot touch.
  3. Check orientation: If all symbols are correct on correct faces, verify no symbol is mirrored.

Eliminate wrong options rather than trying to rotate every option mentally.

Odd One Out — Which Cube is Different?
✓
🧒 Simple Explanation

You're given 4 or 5 cube images and told most of them show the same cube, just rotated differently. One is an impostor — it's a different cube that sneaked in. Your job is to find the fake! Use your cube detective skills: check if any option has a face-symbol pair that breaks the rules established by the others.

📖 Full Explanation

In Odd One Out (or "Which is Different?") questions, most options represent valid rotations of the same cube — but one option has an inconsistency (wrong face position, wrong orientation, or impossible adjacency). Strategy:

  1. Build the opposite pairs from the options that seem consistent.
  2. Check each option against those pairs.
  3. The option that violates the established pairs is the odd one out.

Two Faces Fix the Third Rule: If 2 adjacent faces match their expected positions, the 3rd face is forced — it has no other valid position.

📐 Worked Example

4 cube images. From Cubes 1,2,3 (consistent): ⭐ opp 🌙, 🔴 opp 🔵, 🎯 opp 🎪.

Cube 4 shows: Top=⭐, Front=🌙, Right=🎯.

⭐ and 🌙 are opposite (established from cubes 1,2,3).

Cube 4 shows ⭐ (top) and 🌙 (front) — both visible and adjacent. Contradiction!

Cube 4 is the Odd One Out. ✓

⚠️ Watch Out!

Most common mistake: Students pick the option that "looks most different" rather than checking logically. The odd one out might look almost identical to the others — the difference might be just one symbol facing the wrong way. Be systematic, not visual.
