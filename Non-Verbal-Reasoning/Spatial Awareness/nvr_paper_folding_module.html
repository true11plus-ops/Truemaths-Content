Single Fold — Mirror Basics

🟦 Simple Explanation (Age 9)

Imagine you fold a piece of paper in half — like closing a book. Now poke a hole through it. When you open it back up, the hole appears twice — once where you poked it, and once in the same spot on the other side. The fold is like a mirror: whatever is on one side appears on the other. That's all there is to a single fold!

📘 Full Explanation

A single fold creates two overlapping layers of paper. Any hole punched through both layers will appear symmetrically reflected across the fold line when the paper is unfolded. The hole's distance from the fold line is preserved — the reflected hole appears at the exact same distance on the opposite side. The fold line acts as an axis of symmetry.

  • Horizontal fold → holes reflect vertically (top–bottom)
  • Vertical fold → holes reflect horizontally (left–right)
  • Result: always 2 holes for 1 punch through a single fold
📐 Worked Example 1 — Easy

A square sheet is folded in half horizontally (top half folds down onto bottom half). One hole is punched near the top-right corner of the folded paper. What does the unfolded paper look like?

Start
→
Folded + punched
→
Unfolded result
1

Identify the fold line. The fold is horizontal — a line across the middle. The paper was folded top-down.

2

Find the hole position. The punch is near the top-right of the folded paper. That's close to the fold edge on the right.

3

Mirror the hole. Reflect the hole across the fold line. Top-right → the mirror appears bottom-right, at the same distance from the centre.

4

Answer: Two holes — one near top-right, one near bottom-right, equal distance from the horizontal centre.

⚠️ Watch Out!

Most common mistake: Students place the second hole in the wrong corner (e.g. bottom-left instead of bottom-right). Always ask: "What is the fold direction?" and mirror only across that exact line. A horizontal fold mirrors up-down, NOT diagonally!

📐 Worked Example 2 — Harder

A sheet is folded vertically (right half folds onto left). A hole is punched near the centre-bottom of the folded paper. What does the unfolded paper look like?

0

1

2

3

4

Double Fold — 4 Layers, 4 Holes

🟦 Simple Explanation (Age 9)

Now fold the paper twice — like folding a piece of paper for a note. First fold it in half one way, then fold it in half again another way. Now when you poke a hole, you're going through 4 layers at once! When you unfold, there are 4 holes — like the corners of a square or rectangle. Each fold added a mirror image.

📘 Full Explanation

Two folds create 4 layers (2¹ × 2¹ = 4). Each fold doubles the layers. A hole punched through all 4 layers creates 4 holes when fully unfolded. The positions are determined by applying the Rewind Method: unfold in reverse order, mirroring the hole at each step.

  • Fold 1 (vertical) + Fold 2 (horizontal): 4 holes in a rectangular arrangement
  • Rewind Method: Undo last fold first, mirror hole → undo first fold, mirror both holes
  • Hole count formula: n folds = 2ⁿ holes (when punch goes through all layers, and no overlaps)
📐 Worked Example 1 — Double Fold

Sheet folded vertically first (right onto left), then horizontally (top onto bottom). One hole punched in the top-right of the final folded paper.

0

STEP 0: 2 folds = 4 layers = 4 holes. Eliminate all options that don't show exactly 4 holes.

1

Undo last fold (horizontal): Mirror the hole top-to-bottom. Top-right → also bottom-right. Now we have 2 holes.

2

Undo first fold (vertical): Mirror BOTH holes left-to-right. Top-right → top-left. Bottom-right → bottom-left. Now 4 holes.

3

Answer: 4 holes — one in each corner quadrant. Top-right, top-left, bottom-right, bottom-left.

⚠️ Watch Out!

Most common mistake: Students mirror in the wrong order — they try to undo fold 1 before fold 2. Always undo the last fold first. Think of it like undoing knots: the last one you tied is the first one you untie!

📐 Worked Example 2 — Double Fold, Non-Corner Punch

Sheet folded horizontally first (top onto bottom), then vertically (left onto right). One hole punched near the centre of the folded paper.

0

STEP 0: 2 folds = 4 holes expected. Remove all options with fewer.

1

Undo vertical fold (last): Mirror left-to-right. Centre → same position mirrored. Two holes appear in the centre row.

2

Undo horizontal fold (first): Mirror both holes top-to-bottom. Two centre holes → 4 holes, arranged symmetrically around the sheet centre.

3

Answer: 4 holes arranged symmetrically, one in each quadrant but close to the centre lines.

Practice Questions — Double Fold

StarterQ2.1
A sheet is folded twice (once horizontally, once vertically). One hole is punched. How many holes appear after unfolding?
✅ Correct! Each fold doubles the layers: fold 1 = 2 layers, fold 2 = 4 layers. One punch through 4 layers = 4 holes. This is the key rule: 2ⁿ holes for n folds.
❌ Check your layer count. 2 folds = 2² = 4 layers = 4 holes (assuming the punch goes through all layers and no overlaps). Remember: each fold doubles the layers!
StarterQ2.2
A sheet is folded: first vertically (right onto left), then horizontally (top onto bottom). A hole is punched at the bottom-left corner of the folded paper. After the vertical fold is undone (step 1 of Rewind), where are the holes?
✅ Correct! The last fold was horizontal (top onto bottom), so we undo that first. The hole at bottom-left of the folded paper mirrors to bottom-left on the unfolded top half — giving bottom-left and bottom-right... wait — we undo the horizontal fold: the hole at bottom-left reflects to top-left. But that's two holes in the left column. Then undoing the vertical fold mirrors left-to-right giving all 4. At the intermediate step of undoing only the horizontal fold, bottom-left mirrors to top-left (same column). Then we undo the vertical fold to get all 4.
❌ Remember the order! The last fold was horizontal. Undoing it mirrors the hole top-to-bottom (not left-to-right). So bottom-left becomes top-left. Two holes: top-left and bottom-left. Then undoing the vertical fold mirrors left-to-right to give all 4.
DevelopingQ2.3
A sheet is folded horizontally then vertically. A hole is punched near the top-right corner of the folded paper. Which describes the final hole pattern after full unfolding?
✅ Correct! Rewind: undo vertical fold (last) → top-right mirrors to top-left (2 holes). Undo horizontal fold (first) → both mirror downward: top-right → bottom-right, top-left → bottom-left. Final: 4 holes, one in each corner quadrant.
❌ Apply the Rewind Method. With 2 folds, you always get 4 holes (not 2 or 3). Undo folds in reverse order. Each undo doubles the holes. Top-right corner punch → ends up with one hole near each corner.
SecureQ2.4
Sheet folded: vertically first (left onto right), then horizontally (bottom onto top). Hole punched near the centre of the folded paper's left edge. What is the unfolded result?
✅ Correct! Rewind: undo horizontal fold (last) → hole on left-centre of folded paper mirrors top-to-bottom → 2 holes on left edge (one in upper half, one in lower half). Then undo vertical fold → both left-edge holes mirror to right edge → 4 holes total: 2 on left, 2 on right, at matching heights.
❌ Follow the steps. Undo folds in reverse: last fold first. The fold was horizontal (bottom-top), so undo it by mirroring top-to-bottom — your 1 hole becomes 2 on the left edge. Then undo the vertical fold to mirror those 2 holes across to the right edge, giving 4 total.
MasteryQ2.5
A sheet is folded vertically then horizontally. A hole is punched exactly on the vertical fold line of the twice-folded paper (before the horizontal fold was applied). How many visible holes appear after full unfolding?
✅ Excellent reasoning! The hole lands on the vertical fold line. When we undo the vertical fold, the two copies of the hole at that position are at the same point — they overlap into 1 hole (on the vertical centre). Then undo the horizontal fold — that 1 hole mirrors top-to-bottom, giving 2 holes. So 2 visible holes, both on the vertical centre line — one in the upper half, one in the lower half. The vertical fold overlap halved the count!
❌ Watch for overlaps! A hole punched on the fold line itself means the mirror image lands in the exact same position. When undoing the vertical fold, the 2 holes overlap into 1. Then undoing the horizontal fold makes that 1 hole into 2. Final count: 2 holes, not 4.

Off-Centre Folds — Uneven Spacing

🟦 Simple Explanation (Age 9)

Usually we fold paper exactly in half — right down the middle. But sometimes a fold is made off-centre, like folding 1/3 of the paper over instead of exactly half. This means the two layers are different sizes, and the holes don't end up evenly spaced! The mirror still works, but the distances are different on each side of the fold line.

📘 Full Explanation

In an off-centre fold, the fold line is not at the midpoint of the paper. This has two important effects:

  • Uneven hole distribution: Holes do NOT appear symmetrically about the sheet centre — they are asymmetrically placed.
  • Partial layer coverage: If the fold is smaller than half, part of the paper has only 1 layer. A punch in the single-layer area creates only 1 hole; a punch in the 2-layer area creates 2.
  • Distance preservation: The reflected hole is still at the same distance from the fold line, but the fold line itself is off-centre.
📐 Worked Example 1 — Off-Centre Horizontal Fold

The top 1/3 of a sheet is folded down (onto the bottom 2/3). A hole is punched 1cm from the top edge of the now-folded portion. What does the unfolded result look like?

0

Understand the fold: The fold line is at 1/3 from the top. The top third folds down. The folded paper now has: a 2-layer top section (1/3 height) and a single-layer section below.

1

Locate the hole: 1cm from the top edge of the folded-over portion = 1cm below the fold line (from the inside).

2

Mirror across fold line: The hole is 1cm below the fold line. Its mirror appears 1cm above the fold line in the original sheet = near the very top edge.

3

Answer: Two holes — one near the top edge, one 2cm from the top (both holes are near the top third of the sheet, NOT evenly spaced top and bottom).

⚠️ Watch Out!

Most common mistake: Students assume the holes will be evenly spaced top-and-bottom as in a centre fold. In an off-centre fold, BOTH holes are in the smaller folded portion — they appear close together near the top (or whichever side was folded). Don't spread them out across the whole sheet!

📐 Worked Example 2 — Partial Layer Punch

Top quarter of a sheet is folded down. A hole is punched in the lower three-quarters region (the single-layer area). How many holes after unfolding?

1

Identify layers at punch point: The hole is in the lower three-quarters — that area has only 1 layer (the fold only covered the top quarter).

2

Only 1 layer: The punch goes through just 1 layer → only 1 hole.

3

Answer: 1 hole only, in the lower part of the sheet. The fold had no effect on this region.

Practice Questions — Off-Centre Folds

StarterQ3.1
The top quarter of a sheet is folded down. A hole is punched through the folded (2-layer) area. How many holes appear after unfolding?
✅ Correct! The punch goes through the 2-layer zone, so 2 holes appear. Even though the fold is off-centre, a punch through 2 layers always gives 2 holes. The difference is they won't be evenly spaced across the sheet.
❌ Count the layers at the punch point. The punch is in the folded 2-layer area. Through 2 layers = 2 holes. The off-centre fold changes where the holes appear, not how many when in the 2-layer zone.
StarterQ3.2
The top quarter of a sheet is folded down. A hole is punched in the lower three-quarters region (where there is only 1 layer). How many holes after unfolding?
✅ Correct! The punch is in the single-layer zone. Through 1 layer = 1 hole. The fold didn't create a second layer in that area, so there's nothing to mirror.
❌ Key idea: In an off-centre fold, the lower portion has only 1 layer. A punch there goes through just 1 layer = 1 hole only. Off-centre folds create single-layer zones!
DevelopingQ3.3
The top third of a sheet is folded down. A hole is punched 2cm from the top edge of the folded-over flap (i.e. in the 2-layer zone). After unfolding, the two holes are:
✅ Correct! The fold is off-centre — only the top third folds. Both the hole and its mirror are within that top-third zone (one near the top edge, one near the fold line). They both appear in the upper portion of the sheet, close together — NOT spread evenly top-to-bottom.
❌ Remember: off-centre = uneven. Because only the top third was folded, both holes stay in the top third of the unfolded sheet. They are close together near the top — not spread across the whole sheet like in a half-fold.
SecureQ3.4
The right quarter of a sheet is folded to the left (an off-centre vertical fold). A hole is punched in the right half of the visible sheet — which is the single-layer zone. How many holes appear, and where?
✅ Correct! The punch is in the single-layer zone (right portion, where no fold occurred). Through 1 layer = 1 hole. After unfolding, the hole appears in the left-centre area — the position is on the original sheet's left section where the punch landed. Only 1 hole.
❌ Single layer = single hole. The right portion (where you punched) has only 1 layer. The fold is to the far right. So the punch creates only 1 hole. Remember: partial/off-centre folds mean some areas have fewer layers!
MasteryQ3.5
The top 40% of a sheet is folded down. A hole is punched exactly at the fold line. After unfolding, how many holes are visible and where?
✅ Brilliant! A hole punched exactly on the fold line is punched at the crease. When the paper unfolds, both copies of the hole are at the exact same position — the fold line itself. They overlap completely, leaving just 1 visible hole at the fold line (40% from the top). Classic overlap trap!
❌ Overlap trap! When the punch lands on the fold line, both layers are pierced at the same point. After unfolding, the two holes land on top of each other — only 1 visible hole at the fold line. This is the same overlap principle as in single-fold problems.

Multi-Punch — Independent Symmetry Paths

🟦 Simple Explanation (Age 9)

What if you poke two holes before you unfold? Each hole has its own set of mirror copies — they don't interfere with each other. Think of it like two separate stamps: each stamp makes its own pattern. You track each hole separately through the Rewind Method, then put both patterns together at the end.

📘 Full Explanation

Multiple holes punched through the same folded paper each follow independent symmetry paths. Each punch creates its own reflected set. The key rules:

  • Track each hole separately through the Rewind Method
  • Total holes = sum of all individual hole reflections (minus any overlaps)
  • Overlaps: If two punches reflect onto the same position, they appear as one hole
  • Near-fold-line punches: A hole near the fold line creates its mirror very close to itself — they may appear to cluster
📐 Worked Example — Two Punches, Single Fold

Sheet folded horizontally. Punch A is near the top-left of the folded paper. Punch B is near the top-right. What does the unfolded sheet look like?

1

Track Hole A separately: Near top-left → mirrors to bottom-left. Two holes from A: top-left, bottom-left.

2

Track Hole B separately: Near top-right → mirrors to bottom-right. Two holes from B: top-right, bottom-right.

3

Combine: Total 4 holes — top-left, top-right, bottom-left, bottom-right. One near each corner. (Note: this looks the same as a double fold result, but it came from 2 punches through 1 fold!)

⚠️ Watch Out!

Most common mistake: Students merge the two punches mentally and treat them as one symmetry group. Track each punch independently! A near-fold-line punch creates a cluster of 2 very close holes. A corner punch creates 2 holes far apart. Keep them separate in your mind.

📐 Worked Example 2 — Two Punches, One Near Fold Line

Sheet folded vertically. Punch A is near the top-left corner. Punch B is very close to the fold line (centre of the folded paper). What is the unfolded result?

1

Hole A: Top-left → mirrors left-right → top-right. Two holes from A: top-left and top-right.

2

Hole B: Very close to fold line → its mirror is also very close to fold line. Two holes close together near the vertical centre.

3

Total: 4 holes — top-left, top-right (from A), plus 2 close together near centre (from B). The B holes form a "cluster" near the middle.

Practice Questions — Multi-Punch

StarterQ4.1
A sheet is folded horizontally. Two holes are punched — one near the left side, one near the right side (both in the top part of the folded paper). How many total holes appear after unfolding?
✅ Correct! Each of the 2 punches goes through 2 layers (1 fold), creating 2 holes each. Total = 2 × 2 = 4 holes. Track them separately: left punch → left hole + left mirror. Right punch → right hole + right mirror.
❌ Track each punch separately. 2 punches × 2 layers each = 4 holes total. Each punch creates its own pair. Don't forget to count both the original and the mirrored hole for each punch!
DevelopingQ4.2
Sheet folded vertically. Two holes punched: Hole A near the top-right corner, Hole B directly on the fold line. How many visible holes after unfolding?
✅ Correct! Hole A (top-right corner) → reflects to top-left → 2 holes. Hole B (on the fold line) → its mirror overlaps exactly → 1 hole. Total visible: 2 + 1 = 3 holes. The fold-line punch always collapses to 1 visible hole.
❌ Remember Hole B's special case! Hole B is on the fold line. Its mirror lands in the same spot → they overlap → only 1 visible hole from Hole B. Hole A gives 2 holes normally. Total = 3 visible holes.
SecureQ4.3
Sheet folded horizontally, then vertically (2 folds). Two holes punched: Hole A near the top-right corner of the folded paper, Hole B near the bottom-left corner. How many total holes after unfolding?
✅ Correct! With 2 folds = 4 layers, each punch creates 4 holes. Two punches × 4 holes each = 8 holes. Track each: Hole A (top-right corner of folded paper) → after 2 unfolds → 4 holes near the corners. Hole B (bottom-left corner) → also maps to 4 different symmetric positions. Combined: 8 holes (assuming no overlaps, which is true since the punches are in different corners).
❌ 2 folds = 4 layers. Each punch creates 4 holes (not 2). Two punches = 2 × 4 = 8 holes total. Apply the Rewind Method to each punch independently.
MasteryQ4.4
Sheet folded vertically. Two holes punched very close together near the fold line. After unfolding, how does the hole pattern look?
✅ Correct! Each punch is near the fold line, so each creates 2 holes very close together (near the vertical centre). Two punches × 2 holes each = 4 holes total, but they form two small clusters close to the vertical centre line. This is the "cluster illusion" trap — they might look like fewer holes at first glance!
❌ Near-fold-line means near-centre after unfolding. Each punch near the fold line creates 2 holes very close together near the centre. With 2 such punches, you get 4 holes in two clusters — all near the vertical centre. They won't spread to the corners because the punches weren't near the edges.

Overlap, Cluster & False Symmetry Traps

🟦 Simple Explanation (Age 9)

Sometimes two holes end up in the same spot — they overlap, so you only see one hole instead of two. Sometimes holes are so close together that they look like one big hole — a "cluster." And sometimes a pattern looks symmetrical but actually isn't! These are the traps that trip up even clever students. The fix: count carefully, and always use the Rewind Method rather than guessing from the picture.

📘 Full Explanation

The four major traps in paper folding questions:

  • Overlap trap: Punch on fold line → both copies in same position → 1 visible hole. Multiple punches can also accidentally overlap.
  • Cluster illusion: Holes near the fold line appear very close together. Students miscount them as fewer holes.
  • False symmetry: A pattern that looks symmetrical from a fold might not be — especially with off-centre folds. Check distances carefully.
  • Wrong unfold order: Undoing folds in the wrong sequence gives a completely wrong answer. Always last fold first.
📐 Worked Example — Overlap Trap

Sheet folded vertically then horizontally. Hole A punched near top-right. After one unfold (vertical), it becomes 2 holes (top-right, top-left). After second unfold (horizontal), both become bottom versions. But what if Hole B was also punched near top-left of the folded paper?

1

Track Hole A (top-right): Unfold vertical → top-right + top-left. Unfold horizontal → 4 holes at all corners.

2

Track Hole B (top-left): Unfold vertical → top-left + top-right. Unfold horizontal → 4 holes at all corners again.

3

Combine — CHECK FOR OVERLAPS: Both A and B create holes at the same 4 corner positions! All 8 punches overlap into 4 visible holes. The answer is 4, not 8!

⚠️ Watch Out!

The False Symmetry Trap: In an off-centre fold, the pattern looks like it should be symmetric — but the fold line isn't in the middle, so the hole positions are NOT evenly spaced. Always calculate the actual distance from the fold line rather than assuming equal spacing.

📐 Worked Example 2 — Wrong Unfold Order

Sheet folded: Step 1 = vertical fold. Step 2 = horizontal fold. Hole punched in top-right. Wrong approach vs Correct approach:

❌

Wrong (unfold Step 1 first): Mirror left-right first → top-right + top-left. Then mirror top-bottom → 4 holes. Seems OK, but the positions are calculated incorrectly because the coordinate system is wrong at this stage.

✅

Correct (unfold Step 2 first): Mirror top-bottom → top-right + bottom-right. Then mirror left-right → all 4 corners. This is the same answer here, but in complex cases (off-centre, near edges), wrong order gives wrong positions.

!

Rule: Always undo the LAST fold first. No exceptions.

Practice Questions — Traps & Advanced

DevelopingQ5.1
Sheet folded vertically. Two holes punched: one near the top-left corner, and one near the bottom-left corner of the folded paper. How many holes after unfolding?
✅ Correct! Hole 1 (top-left) → mirrors to top-right. Hole 2 (bottom-left) → mirrors to bottom-right. Total: top-left, top-right, bottom-left, bottom-right = 4 holes. Each punch through 1 fold = 2 holes. Two punches = 4 holes total.
❌ Track each hole. Each punch through 1 fold = 2 holes. Two punches = 4 holes. Top-left becomes top-left + top-right. Bottom-left becomes bottom-left + bottom-right. Total = 4 distinct holes.
SecureQ5.2
Sheet folded vertically. Two holes punched in the folded paper: one near the top-right corner, one near the bottom-right corner. After unfolding, how are the holes arranged?
✅ Correct! A vertical fold mirrors left-right. Top-right → mirrors to top-left. Bottom-right → mirrors to bottom-right... wait — the fold is vertical, so right side was folded onto left. The visible paper after folding shows the left side on top. A punch in the "right corner" of the folded paper is actually in the left region of the original sheet. Mirroring gives one hole on each side. Result: 4 holes, one near each corner.
❌ A vertical fold mirrors left-to-right. Two punches through 2 layers each = 4 total holes. Each punch creates one on each side of the vertical centre. Final result: 4 holes, symmetrically placed, one near each corner.
MasteryQ5.3
Sheet folded: right-quarter folds onto left (off-centre vertical). Two holes punched: Hole A in the 2-layer zone near the fold line, Hole B in the single-layer zone (far left). Which answer correctly shows the unfolded result?
✅ Expert-level reasoning! Hole A is in the 2-layer zone near the fold line → creates 2 holes close together near the right portion of the sheet (where the fold was). Hole B is in the single-layer zone (left side) → creates only 1 hole on the left. Total: 3 visible holes. The off-centre fold and single-layer zone make this a complex case!
❌ Break it down by zone. Hole A (2-layer zone, near fold line) = 2 holes close together near the right. Hole B (single-layer zone) = 1 hole on left. Total = 3 holes. The off-centre fold means NOT all areas have 2 layers — and a punch near the fold line creates a cluster, not widely spaced holes.
MasteryQ5.4
After unfolding, a student sees what appears to be 3 holes — but the paper was folded twice and punched once. What is the most likely explanation?
✅ Correct! Two folds normally create 4 holes. If only 3 are visible, the most likely cause is that the punch was placed on or very close to one of the fold lines. When a hole lands on a fold line, its two mirror copies overlap — reducing the visible count by 1. So 4 potential holes → 2 overlap → 3 visible.
❌ Think about overlaps. 2 folds = 4 holes normally. If you see 3, one pair must have overlapped. This happens when the punch was on a fold line. The two copies at that position combine into 1. This is the overlap trap — it's the most common reason for "missing" holes in exam questions.
