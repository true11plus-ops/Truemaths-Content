1. The Core Trick — What Is a Hidden Word?

✓
🧒 Simple Explanation
Imagine words in a sentence are like train carriages. A hidden word is a secret word that starts at the END of one carriage and finishes at the START of the next one. It crosses the gap between two words. You find it by looking at where two words join together — the ends and beginnings glue into a secret word!
📖 Full Explanation
A hidden word is a real word formed by taking the final letters of one word and the opening letters of the next word (or sometimes the word after that) in a sentence. The hidden word always crosses a word boundary — it cannot be entirely inside one word. GL Assessment uses three main structures: 2+2 (two letters from each word), 3+1 (three from the first, one from the next), and 1+3 (one from the first, three from the next).

⚠️ Watch Out!
The hidden word is NEVER entirely inside one word. If you think you see a word sitting completely inside one word (like "HOP" inside "SHOPPING"), that does not count — it must cross the boundary between two words. Every time!

2. Systematic Boundary Scanning

✓
🧒 Simple Explanation
Don't jump randomly around the sentence! Start at the first two words, check them, then move to the next pair. It's like reading a number line — you go left to right, one gap at a time. You never skip a gap without checking it. Think: Gap 1 → check. Gap 2 → check. Gap 3 → check.
📖 Full Explanation
Systematic boundary scanning means checking every word boundary in the sentence in order, from left to right. At each boundary, try all three structures (2+2, 3+1, 1+3) before moving on. Do NOT rely on visual guessing or reading for meaning — the answer may be at any boundary in the sentence, including the last one.

3. Search Order — Try 2+2 First

✓
🧒 Simple Explanation
You have three tools: 2+2, 3+1, and 1+3. Use them in the right order to save time! The 2+2 trick works most often, so try it first at every boundary. If it fails, try 3+1 (three from the first word). Last resort: 1+3 (one from the first, three from the next). Think of it like trying the front door, then the back door, then the window!
📖 Full Explanation
GL Assessment data shows that 2+2 structures appear most frequently, followed by 3+1, then 1+3. By testing in this priority order at each boundary, you maximise speed. If you find a valid word with the first structure tried, verify it appears in the answer options before committing — then move on. The priority order is: 2+2 → 3+1 → 1+3.

TORE is a real word (past tense of tear). Check it's in the options — done!

⚠️ Watch Out!
Don't try all three structures at every boundary before moving on — that's too slow. If 2+2 gives a real word, check the options immediately. Only try 3+1 if 2+2 fails, and only try 1+3 if 3+1 also fails. Speed matters in the exam.

4. The Option-First Strategy

✓
🧒 Simple Explanation
Instead of starting with the sentence, start with the answer choices! Look at each answer option and ask: "Does this word appear, crossing a gap, somewhere in the sentence?" It's like being a detective who already knows the suspect — now you just need to prove it. This can be MUCH faster than scanning every boundary.
📖 Full Explanation
The option-first (reverse search) strategy involves reading the answer options first, then hunting for each option's letter sequence crossing a word boundary in the sentence. For example, if an option is LACE, you scan the sentence for "...L ACE..." or "...LA CE..." or "...LAC E..." straddling a boundary. This is fastest when the options are distinct and the sentence is long.
✏️ Worked Example — Option-First in Action
The clue leads all great detectives to success.
1
Options: DEAL, LEAD, SALE, SEAM. Read option 1: DEAL. Hunt for D-E-A-L straddling a boundary.
2
Scan sentence: "clue | leads" → clueD = no D at end. "leads | all" → leaDS → DS+AL = DSAL ✗. "leaD | S(all)" → D+SAL? "leaDS | ALL": wait: "leads | all" last 2 = DS, first 2 = AL → DSAL ✗. Hmm.
3
Try option 2: LEAD. Hunt L-E-A-D: "clue | leads" → UE+LE = UELE ✗. "the | clue" → HE+CL = HECL ✗. No boundary gives LEAD spanning it.
4
Try option 3: SALE. S-A-L-E: scan for ...S | ALE... → "leads | all" → DS+AL nope. "success" → no. Hmm.
5
Try option 4: SEAM. S-E-A-M: "leads | all" → DS+AL ✗. "success" — no. Hmm.
✅ Option-first is fastest when the options are clearly different words — you eliminate 3 wrong ones rapidly!
⚠️ Watch Out!
Quick rejection rule: if an option has no vowel, reject it instantly — it can't be a real word. Also, if ALL letters of the option are inside a single word in the sentence (not crossing a gap), eliminate it immediately. Don't waste time checking every structure for an impossible option.

5. Common Traps — Don't Fall For These!

✓
🧒 Simple Explanation
GL questions have four sneaky traps. The "inside-word" trap makes you think a word is hidden when it's actually all inside one word. The "near-word" trap gives you something that SOUNDS like a word but isn't. The "double-valid" trap has TWO words crossing boundaries — but only one is in the options. And the "all-look-right" trap makes all the answers seem possible! Check the options AND the boundaries before committing.
📖 Full Explanation
The four main trap types: (1) Inside-word illusion — a word appears fully within one word, not spanning a boundary. (2) Looks-valid-but-isn't — a letter combination sounds phonetically plausible but isn't a standard word. (3) Double-valid-word trap — two boundaries each yield a real word, but only one appears in the options (option validation discipline). (4) Option misdirection — all four options look like plausible answers; you must verify each against the sentence.
✏️ Trap 1: Inside-Word Illusion

✅ Rule: If you can underline the word WITHOUT crossing a space, it doesn't count!
⚠️ Watch Out! — All 4 Traps Listed
Trap 1: Inside-word — must cross a space/boundary. Always.
Trap 2: Near-word — LEFI, NGCE look letter-ish but aren't words. Trust your vocabulary.
Trap 3: Double-valid — when two boundaries work, check BOTH against options before committing.
Trap 4: Option misdirection — don't pick the "nicest looking" word; prove it with boundary evidence.
